## 1. Estrutura do quadro de TCO (Layered TCO Framework)

### 1.1 Definição de TCO tradicional e de TCO baseado no ciclo de vida
- "Traditional TCO focuses primarily on the direct costs of acquiring, implementing, operating and maintaining technology. In contrast, a life cycle-based TCO expands this perspective by considering the organizational investments required for the planning, implementation, governance, operation, continuous improvement and long-term sustainability of AI-enabled systems." (p. 3–4)
- Repetido na seção de política: "A life cycle-based TCO widens this lens to account for the organizational investments required across the full life of an AI-enabled system: planning, implementation, governance, operations, continuous improvement and long-term sustainability." (p. 8)
- "These organizational investments listed above form the interconnected dimensions of the Layered TCO Framework (Figure 1) and collectively determine whether AI investments deliver long-term value." (p. 4)

### 1.2 As sete dimensões (texto corrido)
- "The framework organizes AI-related costs across seven dimensions: governance; applications; infrastructure; data; cybersecurity; workforce capacity (including teacher professional development); and ongoing operations." (p. 6)

### 1.3 Figura 1 (p. 11) — conteúdo integral de cada camada
Título: "Figure 1: Layered TCO Framework: The following layered framework illustrates how AI implementation introduces interconnected cost dimensions across education systems" (p. 11).

**Layer 1 — GOVERNANCE, POLICY, AND LIFECYCLE PLANNING** (p. 11)
- "Governance structures, policy development, ethical review, procurement guidance"
- "Monitoring routines, legal review, and long-term lifecycle planning"
- "Governance increasingly becomes part of the operational cost structure for sustainable AI implementation."

**Layer 2 — AI APPLICATIONS, USER EXPERIENCE, AND ADOPTION** (p. 11)
- "AI-enabled tools, applications, subscriptions, and user experiences most visible to educators, students, and administrators"
- "Enters education systems through instructional support, productivity tools, automation, and day-to-day workflows"
- "Visible AI applications represent only a portion of the broader lifecycle investment required to sustain AI responsibly"
- "Requires continuous evaluation across governance, workforce capacity, cybersecurity, infrastructure, funding, and equity throughout the AI lifecycle."

**Layer 3 — INFRASTRUCTURE AND DEVICES** (p. 11)
- "Increased demands on infrastructure and bandwidth as cloud ecosystems, device usage, cybersecurity monitoring, real-time analytics, and AI-enabled instructional services expand"
- "AI accelerates existing technology pressures related to cloud dependency, device capability, staffing, cybersecurity, and operational scalability."

**Layer 4 — DATA INTEGRATION AND SYSTEMS** (p. 11)
- "Relies on interoperability, rostering, identity systems, analytics platforms, learning systems, and student information systems"
- "Integration and data management increase operational complexity and support requirements"
- "As AI matures, systems may need to invest in organizing and maintaining institutional knowledge assets and educational knowledge commons—policies, procedures, curriculum resources, operational guidance, and historical documentation."

**Layer 5 — CYBERSECURITY, PRIVACY, AND COMPLIANCE** (p. 11)
- "AI expands the importance of identity management, monitoring, third-party risk management, privacy protections, compliance obligations, and operational resilience"
- "AI acts as both a productivity tool and a cybersecurity risk multiplier"
- "As AI agents perform tasks on behalf of users, systems may need to reconsider identity, access, and permission-management models to ensure secure and governed use."

**Layer 6 — PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT AND WORKFORCE CAPACITY** (p. 11)
- "Workforce readiness significantly affects TCO"
- "Introductory AI exposure evolves into sustained demands for coaching, governance training, technical support, leadership development, and role-specific implementation capacity"
- "Workforce readiness remains one of the largest barriers to sustainable AI implementation."

**Layer 7 — OPERATIONS, SUPPORT, AND SUSTAINABILITY** (p. 11)
- "Requires ongoing technical support, monitoring, vendor management, maintenance, operational oversight, evaluation, and sustainability planning"
- "Operational obligations continue throughout the lifecycle of AI-enabled systems."

**KEY TAKEAWAY** (p. 11): "AI implementation creates ecosystem costs across seven interconnected layers. Sustainable AI in education requires strategic investment across the entire lifecycle—from governance and adoption to operations and long-term sustainability."

### 1.4 Como usar o quadro ("About the framework", p. 10)
- "The framework shows how these investments function together to support responsible, sustainable and scalable AI implementation, rather than representing independent cost categories."
- "While each layer represents a distinct area of investment, the framework emphasizes the principle that sustainable implementation depends on how these layers interact across the full AI life cycle."
- "The framework can be used as a planning and assessment tool, to identify readiness gaps, clarify investment priorities and anticipate costs that would otherwise remain hidden during procurement or early implementation."
- "Because education systems differ in governance structures, digital maturity, infrastructure and resources, it is intended to be adapted to local contexts while maintaining a consistent life cycle perspective."

### 1.5 Camada "abaixo" das sete: condições de base
O texto (não a Figura 1) trata conectividade, dispositivos e energia como a primeira camada do ciclo de vida em sistemas com poucos recursos:
- "For many systems, the life cycle cost of AI begins below the software line, with the foundational conditions that wealthier systems take for granted" (título, p. 7)
- Bangladesh: "Where connectivity, devices and reliable power are themselves the first and most unequal cost, a TCO framework that assumes the existence of a mature digital ecosystem would have limited immediate value. For such systems, the earliest layer of the life cycle – foundational infrastructure and access – is the largest cost and the most consequential for equity." (p. 7)
- Restrições de base listadas no resumo: "infrastructure, connectivity and capacity, including reliable access to electricity, internet connectivity, digital devices, technical capacity and ongoing support." (p. 2)

- Trajetória de adoção descrita (não como estágios formais): "What began in some education systems as experimentation with early AI-enabled tools is evolving into large-scale integration across learning systems, cloud ecosystems, cybersecurity environments, identity infrastructure, analytics platforms and administrative operations." - **Antes da implantação**: "life cycle-based TCO planning may play an especially important role in helping education systems evaluate long-term feasibility, sustainability and educational value before implementation." (p. 4)
- **Piloto**: "Historically, many education systems have evaluated technology investments primarily through acquisition costs, pilot funding or short-term implementation budgets." (p. 9); "Low-cost pilots, freemium models and embedded AI capabilities may create the perception that AI implementation is relatively inexpensive or even free." (p. 9); "limited pilot programmes that cannot scale sustainably across the broader education system" (p. 12).
- **Expansão / escala**: "However, as implementation expands, education systems may experience increasing operational dependency on cloud-service ecosystems, interoperability infrastructure, identity-management systems, cybersecurity protections and ongoing workforce support." - **Investimento de capital e cauda do ciclo de vida**: "Investment at scale carries its own life cycle tail" (título, p. 8); "the visible capital cost is the beginning of the obligation, not the end of it." (p. 8)
- **Saída / descomissionamento**: não consta como estágio nem como custo de saída. O mais próximo: preservar a capacidade de mudar de rumo — "preserve its authority to set terms and to change course" (p. 13); "one way a system pays to preserve its ability to decide later" (p. 8); portabilidade de dados e interoperabilidade com fornecedores como condição de sustentabilidade (p. 12).

## 3. Como a IA é obtida e paga

### 3.1 Custo distribuído e embutido
- "Increasingly, AI capabilities are being woven into existing digital ecosystems rather than entering education systems as sole standalone tools. This fundamentally changes how education systems experience the TCO. Rather than appearing as a single procurement line item, AI-related costs may become distributed across multiple areas, including: cloud-service environments; licensing structures; identity and interoperability systems; cybersecurity protections; governance and data protection processes; continuous professional learning programmes; operational support models; vendor-management responsibilities; and ongoing evaluation activities." (p. 6)
- "As AI capabilities become increasingly embedded within existing technology ecosystems, the true costs of AI are becoming less visible, more distributed and significantly more complex to evaluate." (p. 3)
- "More and more, AI-enabled environments are introducing embedded and distributed costs" (p. 6)

### 3.2 Custo por uso (consumo, tokens, inferência)
- "AI may also introduce variable, usage-based costs, including inference, compute and token consumption (World Bank et al., 2026). This means the cost does not end at purchase: every use of the system draws on computing capacity, and Member States are billed for that use as it accumulates. As AI use scales, these recurring costs should be monitored and incorporated into ongoing life cycle planning." (p. 6)
- Chamadas de API: não consta explicitamente (apenas "inference, compute and token consumption").

### 3.3 Licenças e assinaturas
- "licensing structures" como uma das áreas de custo distribuído (p. 6).
- Figura 1, camada 2: "AI-enabled tools, applications, subscriptions, and user experiences" (p. 11).
- Licenças não usadas: "Spending without the capacity to sustain and integrate technology produces waste, not value: UNESCO's 2023 GEM Report found that around two-thirds of education software licences in the United States of America went unused (Global Education Monitoring Report Team, 2023)." (p. 3)
- Proporção do custo inicial: "The UNESCO's 2023 GEM Report found that initial investment typically represents a quarter or less of technology's eventual cost (Global Education Monitoring Report Team, 2023). What AI changes is the scale of what remains hidden, and how much of it accrues only once a system is in use." (p. 2)
- Preço por usuário / por licença (per-seat): não consta como modelo de preço. Os únicos números por pessoa são o caso nigeriano (US$30 por professor por ano, ver 3.5) e a contagem de 165.000 licenças no Cazaquistão (ver 3.4).

### 3.4 Gratuito, doado, freemium, pilotos de baixo custo
- Título: "The perception that AI can be free deserves particular scrutiny, because free acquisition rarely means free ownership" (p. 7)
- Cazaquistão: "In Kazakhstan, 165,000 licences for an education version of a major AI system are being distributed at no charge for three years, fully financed by private partners instead of the state budget, with the ministry as recipient and not a purchaser. The acquisition cost is zero, but the life cycle costs are not." (p. 7) "The system must still provide training, secure and legally compliant data environments, as well as integration (Satubaldina, 2026). It faces the harder question of what happens when the free period ends and a large body of educators has come to depend on a tool the state never bought and a foreign provider supplies. A donated or free-tier tool bypasses the point at which conditions are normally set, and the costs it defers do not disappear." (p. 8)
- "Several dynamics compound the difficulty. Low-cost pilots, freemium models and embedded AI capabilities may create the perception that AI implementation is relatively inexpensive or even free." (p. 9)
- Pergunta 2 (p. 13): "Where AI ae [sic] made available for free, through donations, free tiers or embedded features, how can an education system account for the costs it defers, and preserve its authority to set terms and to change course?"
- "Pilotos gratuitos" oferecidos por fornecedores: o termo não consta; consta "Low-cost pilots" (p. 9) e o caso de licenças doadas (p. 7–8).

### 3.5 Patrocínio e desenho de baixo custo
- Nigéria (Naija Teacher AI): "a national initiative of the Teachers Registration Council of Nigeria in collaboration with a technology partner. The initiative aims to train 1.5 million licensed teachers and reach more than 30 million students through an offline-first, mobile-ready and multilingual platform, on a low-cost sponsorship model of around US$30 per teacher each year. The design choices are TCO choices: offline-first to reduce dependence on connectivity; mobile-first to avoid device costs; and workforce capacity, not software licensing, as the central investment. It is a reminder that life cycle costs can be lowered by design, and that a system with limited resources can plan deliberately for sustainability." (p. 7)

### 3.6 Construção própria / soberana e investimento de capital
- Países Baixos: "The Kingdom of the Netherlands is funding a sovereign national language model, built by public research and education bodies, explicitly to reduce reliance on non-European providers and to keep sensitive data within the national legal jurisdiction. While the investment is modest by commercial standards, the principle is important: dependency is itself a life cycle cost, and so is the work of avoiding it. Building public capacity and meeting regulatory obligations in-house rather than through a vendor, is one way a system pays to preserve its ability to decide later. Retaining that control is itself a life cycle investment." (p. 8)
- Senegal: "Senegal's Digital Strategy for Education 2025-2029, costed at 130 billion CFA francs (about US$233 million), directs major public spending precisely at the life cycle layers: training 105,000 teachers and administrators, with dedicated attention to cybersecurity and data protection, alongside device distribution. It is candid about there being connectivity gaps and that filling them requires hybrid solutions (Ecofin Agency, 2025)." (p. 8)
- Brasil: "Brazil's national AI plan, at around 23 billion reais (about US$4 billion), invests in sovereign infrastructure including a supercomputer and a government cloud. Brazilian analysts observe that such capital investments demand robust and recurring maintenance, updating and cybersecurity, or they become underused and outdated (UNCTAD, 2024)." (p. 8)

## 7. Recomendações explícitas para formuladores de políticas

"Suggestions for policy action" (p. 12–13). Introdução: "The following suggestions operationalize the seven layers of the Layered TCO Framework and translate them into practical policy actions for education systems. Together, they emphasize that sustainable AI implementation depends on coordinated investments across governance, workforce, infrastructure, cybersecurity, operations and continuous improvement rather than on technology acquisition alone." (p. 12)

1. **"Adopt Life cycle-Based TCO models"** — "Education systems should move beyond evaluating AI based solely on acquisition price. Procurement and investment planning should incorporate governance, cybersecurity, workforce, operational and life cycle sustainability costs." / "The growing use of AI may also require education systems to reevaluate interoperability strategies. Historically, interoperability efforts focus primarily on moving data into applications and vendor platforms. Increasingly, AI-enabled environments depend on the ability to securely and programmatically access, retrieve and utilize data across systems, making data portability and vendor interoperability important considerations for long-term sustainability." (p. 12)
2. **"Build cross-functional governance structures"** — "AI governance should involve collaboration across a number of interconnected areas: technology leadership; curriculum and instruction; cybersecurity; legal and compliance; procurement; operations; and executive leadership." (p. 12)
3. **"Treat workforce capacity as a core TCO component"** — "Education systems should move beyond introductory AI awareness towards sustained workforce readiness through role-specific training, operational support, leadership development and AI literacy." (p. 12)
4. **"Integrate cybersecurity into life cycle planning"** — "AI should increasingly be treated as a cybersecurity and operational risk multiplier. Life cycle-based TCO models should incorporate identity management, monitoring, third-party risk management, privacy safeguards and operational resilience planning." (p. 12)
5. **"Incorporate sustainability and capacity into TCO planning"** — "Policy-makers should evaluate whether education systems have the governance, infrastructure, workforce and operational capacity required to sustain AI responsibly over time." (p. 12)
6. **"Incorporate equity and scalability into TCO planning"** — "Equity considerations should include whether access to AI-enabled tools and services depends on family-provided solutions, uneven local capacity or limited pilot programmes that cannot scale sustainably across the broader education system. Without long-term planning, AI implementation may unintentionally expand existing inequities." (p. 12)
7. **"Develop monitoring and evaluation mechanisms"** — "Education systems should establish mechanisms to measure organizational impact, sustainability, equity outcomes, operational effectiveness and long-term implementation quality." (p. 13)

Outras afirmações de cunho recomendatório fora dessa seção:
- "As AI use scales, these recurring costs should be monitored and incorporated into ongoing life cycle planning." (p. 6)
- "These challenges show why AI readiness is necessary beyond AI adoption and must include: the governance, infrastructure, workforce capacity and institutional conditions that enable sustainable AI implementation" (p. 3)
- "Organizations must account not only for AI tools themselves, but also for the investments in: governance; infrastructure; the education workforce capacity; the professional development of both the teachers and the technical, administrative and leadership staff who govern and sustain AI; cybersecurity; and the life cycle investments required to implement, support and sustain them." (p. 3)
- Conclusão: "The central policy challenge is whether education systems can sustainably govern, support, secure and maintain AI-enabled environments over time." (p. 13); "Ultimately, sustainable AI depends on the long-term investment required to support education as a human-centred common good." (p. 13)

Constatações de contexto úteis para justificar perguntas:
- "education systems may be adopting AI faster than they can sustainably govern, fund, secure, support and scale it over time." (p. 2)
- Governança que ficou para trás: "AI implementation has outpaced the development of foundational governance structures such as acceptable use policies, assessment and academic integrity frameworks, parent communication strategies, and age-appropriate usage guidelines. The result can be inconsistency in classroom practices, educator uncertainty, student misuse, conflicting expectations and increased operational risk." (p. 2–3)
- "AI adoption does not necessarily equal operational readiness." (p. 9); "Adoption, in other words, does not equal readiness." (p. 7)
- Equidade: "Without sustained investments in workforce readiness, infrastructure, data governance and support systems, AI initiatives risk benefiting only those schools and learners with the greatest resources –– thus widening existing digital and educational divides rather than reducing them." (p. 3)
- "The long-term success of AI in education is determined less by the selection of AI tools and more by the organizational capacity required to govern, support, secure and continuously improve them." (p. 3)

### 9.1 O que a ferramenta tem e o documento não traz (não consta no documento)
- Modelo de quatro fases (contexto, piloto, implantação, sustentabilidade e expansão) e a distribuição de camadas por fase (CAMADAS_FASE). O documento não associa camadas a fases.
- Regras do piloto: critérios de sucesso e linha de base, critérios de parada, destino dos dados e materiais (r-sucesso, r-parada, r-ativos).
- Acordo de tratamento de dados sem uso para treinar modelos (r-acordo); avaliação de impacto sobre proteção de dados (i-dpia); finalidade e base legal (i-finalidade).
- Supervisão humana, contestação e correção de erros, rastreabilidade de classificações e previsões (i-supervisao, i-contestacao, i-rastreio). O documento só menciona que a IA gera "inferences, predictions and profiles about learners" (p. 2).
- Testes adversariais, filtros de conteúdo gerado (i-adversarial, i-geracao).
- Acessibilidade para estudantes com deficiência (i-acessib).
- Rotatividade das equipes (i-rotatividade).
- Reavaliação a cada mudança relevante (s-mudanca); o documento só diz que a IA é atualizada continuamente e muda de comportamento após o contrato (p. 2).
- Custo de saída e plano de transição (s-saida; K6.3 em custos.js).
- Cláusulas contratuais de dados, mudança, desempenho e saída (i-contrato).
- Toda a árvore K1–K6 de custos.js com quantificação (licenças, consumo por chamada e tokens, GPUs, RAG, embeddings, hospedagem, salários, testes de aceitação, manutenção evolutiva, saída). O documento não quantifica nada por camada, não menciona RAG, embeddings, GPUs, ajuste fino, arquiteturas SaaS/API/pesos abertos, inflação, câmbio, cenários ou horizonte de anos. Os únicos números do documento são os casos nacionais (p. 7–8) e as duas estatísticas do GEM 2023 (p. 2, p. 3).

### 9.2 O que o documento traz e a ferramenta não cobre (ou cobre pouco)
- **Energia elétrica confiável** como condição de base (p. 2, p. 7, p. 13). A ferramenta não pergunta sobre energia.
- **Shadow AI** como custo oculto e risco (p. 9).
